Passenger Experience
The challenge

Riga is growing as a key Northern European hub. How do we make waiting time more enjoyable and lift passenger satisfaction?

My approach

I mapped the end-to-end passenger journey through the terminal to find the moments where waiting turns into frustration — then explored service concepts that give those moments purpose.

What I did
01Passenger journey mapping & user personas
02Service safaris & social media research
03Surveys & interviews
04Prototyping & recommendations

*Data triangulation

Insights
Basic needs went unmet

Charging, seating and comfort. The services were there, but invisible or hard to find.

Staying close to the gate

Most passengers stay close to the boarding gate to avoid missing their flight.

Cabin luggage limits passenger freedom

It's seen as a barrier to freely exploring the airport, making simple things — visiting the toilets, eating or shopping — less comfortable and more stressful.

*79% of surveyed passengers prioritised charging, ranking it even above food.

THE LOOP

Passengers arrive at the gate, sit, scroll, wait. It's a cycle of low movement and low engagement — passengers describe themselves as “bored”, and satisfaction is lower than it needs to be.

The solution

RIX PowerCart

RIX PowerCart breaks the loop! Enabling easy movement, access to information, and physical support. A mobile service station that brings the airport experience to passengers, making existing services more accessible rather than adding new ones.

It combines real-time flight updates, gate navigation, charging stations, secure luggage storage, AI assistance, service discovery, passenger activity heatmaps, and food ordering into one integrated touchpoint. The solution reduces travel stress, improves service accessibility, and delivers a more seamless and personalised airport experience.

“This cart will help de-anchor passengers, becoming the key artefact that connects everything in the airport.”

Senior Strategic Designer, Lufthansa Innovation Hub

Reflection

This project reframed how I think about "solving" a problem. My first instinct was to design something new for passengers — but the research kept pointing back to services that already existed and simply weren't reaching people at the right moment. The strongest move wasn't adding, it was connecting. If I were to take it further, the next step would be to test the PowerCart in the terminal once the Baltic railway is completed.
