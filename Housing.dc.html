The problem

Tallinn's housing market is neither affordable nor sustainable — and secure housing is slipping out of reach for younger generations.

With 96% of homes privately owned, little regulation, and one of the highest rental costs in Europe, pressure keeps building — and a projected 20% population increase by 2040 will only intensify it.

My approach

Throughout the project, I used systems thinking and futures thinking to move beyond today's challenges and explore what Tallinn's housing system could become.

Our research combined stakeholder interviews, co-design workshops, systems mapping, layered analysis, future scenarios, and backcasting. These methods helped reveal how policies, market dynamics, social trust, and civic participation interact—and where meaningful interventions could create long-term change.

Solution

Housing Lab — a shared platform that brings residents, the city, and experts together to co-create alternative housing.

It is a civic platform that brings together residents, city officials, developers, financial institutions, and housing experts to co-create affordable and community-led housing solutions.

The platform is built around three pillars:

  • Awareness – Inspiring new ways of thinking through events, campaigns, and real-life housing examples.
  • Ideation – Supporting residents to co-design and test new housing initiatives.
  • Navigation – Providing expert guidance and practical roadmaps to turn ideas into reality.

By connecting people, knowledge, and resources, Housing Lab creates the conditions for a more diverse, inclusive, and sustainable housing system.

Outcome

The project was well received by the Tallinn Strategy Centre, highlighting the need for a platform that supports collaboration around alternative housing. It was later recognised with the Best Socially Minded Project of the Year award at the EKA Design Showcase 2025. While still a concept, Housing Lab provides a strong starting point for future experimentation and community-led housing initiatives across Tallinn.

Reflection

This project reinforced the value of designing for systems rather than individual services. The biggest opportunity wasn't creating another housing model, but helping people understand, imagine, and navigate alternatives that had previously felt out of reach.
